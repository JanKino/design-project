# 05 — Target group and market potential

> **Question answered here:** How big is the target group and the market? What do smokers need from a cessation app, and who is willing to pay for one?
> Scope agreed with the team: **global**, with Belgium and the EU as examples. Types: `[official stats]` `[survey]` `[peer-reviewed]` `[market report]` `[vendor]` `[news]`. **Market-research reports are low reliability**: estimates differ by a factor of three depending on what counts as an "app". Use ranges, not single figures.

## Takeaways for our direction

1. **The target group is huge and mostly unsupported.** There are 1.2 billion tobacco users worldwide and more than 100 million vapers. In England about 4 in 10 quit attempts are made with no aid at all. Low-cost digital support therefore has a large potential reach.
2. **Most smokers are not "ready" at a given moment.** In Belgium 39% plan to quit within a year, so the majority do not. A phase-based app that also serves the *motivational* phase (HAPA pre-intenders) addresses a segment most apps ignore.
3. **Consumers pay little; payers pay more.** Stated consumer willingness to pay is a few dollars once. Value is captured by insurers (German DiGA, average ~€361), employers (Pelago) and public health bodies (UK councils buying Smoke Free premium). In Belgium the realistic partners are Tabakstop and tabacologist care, not app sales.
4. **What smokers say they want matches our direction:** personalisation they control, simplicity, varied content, interactivity, privacy, inclusivity and positive framing. Retention is the core market problem: typical health apps keep ~4% of users after 15 days.
5. **We cannot assume a wearable.** Wearable ownership in Europe lags the US, which confirms the modular-data decision (G5).

## Target-group segments (for a Miro persona cluster)

| Segment | HAPA phase | Size signal | Main need | Fitting S2 sub-models |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ambivalent smoker | Pre-intender (motivational) | ~61% of BE smokers not planning to quit within a year (M4) | Non-judgemental reflection; no pressure | MI chatbot, decisional balance, CO/health feedback |
| Ready to quit | Intender (volitional) | 39% plan to quit (M4) | A concrete plan, medication info | Action and coping planning, quit date, medication support, buddy |
| Recently quit | Actor (early abstinence) | ~half of EU smokers have tried (M3) | Craving help at the right moment | JITAI module, exercise/visuospatial blocks, ACT vs CBT coping |
| Lapsed | Recovery | Most attempts end in a lapse (Shiffman et al., 1996) | No guilt; restart quickly | Slip-recovery flow, recovery self-efficacy, plan revision |
| Dual user / vaper | Any | >100M vapers (M2); e-cigs in 40% of English attempts (M5) | Clear stance on e-cigs | E-cig switching or tapering path (design decision, see [06](06_other_findings.md)) |
