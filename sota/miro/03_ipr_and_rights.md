# 03 — Intellectual property, licences and legal rights

> **Question answered here:** Which patents, copyrights, licences and regulations affect a modular, learning, chatbot-based cessation app, and what do we have to do about them during the project and after it?
> Scope agreed with the team: IPR in a broad sense: patents, copyright and content licences, open-source licences, trademarks, **plus** regulation (EU MDR, GDPR, AI Act) and platform rules.
> **Not legal advice.** Patent statuses come from Google Patents listings, and claim scope was not analysed. Check with the UGent Tech Transfer office before any commercial step. Types: `[patent]` `[copyright]` `[open licence]` `[trademark]` `[regulation]` `[guidance]` `[platform rule]`.

## Takeaways for our direction

1. **Patents: some overlap in concept, low risk for a student prototype.** The closest patents (IBM; an eHealth RL application) claim ML that maps interventions to behaviour-change states and learns from feedback. Our hierarchical, ontology-based selector should be documented as our own design, with a dated design log as prior art. A freedom-to-operate check is only needed before commercialisation.
2. **Building on ontologies is legally easy; content needs care.** BCIO and BCTO are CC BY 4.0, so we can reuse them with attribution. Techniques (ACT, MI, implementation intentions) are ideas and cannot be owned, but *texts* (manuals, app scripts, questionnaires) can be. Write our own content and check the licence of each questionnaire.
3. **The intended purpose decides whether we are a medical device.** "Track and support a healthier lifestyle" = not a medical device. "Treat nicotine dependence / predict relapse" = medical device software, typically class IIa under MDR Rule 11. During the project we present the app as a research prototype and wellbeing tool, and we avoid therapeutic claims.
4. **GDPR is the real constraint.** Wearable, EMA and smoking data are health data (Art. 9). We need explicit consent per data source, data minimisation and a DPIA. Our *modular, opt-in data* design fits this well.
5. **AI Act: a transparency duty now, high-risk only if we become a device.** Art. 50 (users must know they are talking to an AI) applies to the chatbot from 2 Aug 2026. Our explainability principle goes further than the law requires, and that is a selling point.

## Recommended actions (for the board)

1. Keep a **licence register** (ontology, questionnaires, libraries, models) in the repo.
2. Write all user-facing content ourselves. Tag each item with its BCTO ID and source.
3. Use **"wellbeing / research prototype"** language in the app and the study materials. No cure or treatment claims.
4. Plan the **ethics + GDPR** package for the user study early: DPIA, consent per data source, EU hosting.
5. Add an **"I am an AI"** disclosure and a "why am I seeing this?" link to every chatbot screen.
6. Check UGent rules on student IP and the release licence with the course team.
